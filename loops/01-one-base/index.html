Palimpsest · loop 01 · Evo 2 7B, computed live

Change one base. What does Evo 2 notice?

Before you play
Why care
Evo 2 is fed DNA one letter at a time. Nothing in its input marks codons, genes or strands.
Testing
When one base changes, what happens to Evo 2's surprise: at that base, next to it, and further along?
What changes
One base, swapped, inserted or deleted. Everything else is identical, and scoring the same sequence twice gives identical numbers.
Watch
The Δ track after the edit, inside and outside the shaded open reading frames.
Possible results
Only the edited base moves · a nearby patch moves, then settles · a shift that lasts until the reading frame ends · different kinds of swap behave differently. Each would mean Evo 2 is tracking something different.
connecting…
  1. Move: drag along the long track, click a letter in the 30-base lens, or use ← → (Shift jumps 10). Everything updates as you move.
  2. Edit: Swap, Insert before, or Delete the chosen base, in the panel below.
  3. Compare: A is the original, B your edit, Δ the difference, aligned even after insertions and deletions. Hover any bar for its value.
  4. Stack edits: "Keep B as the new baseline", then edit again.
  5. Window: change start and length, then Score window. Shorter windows score faster; Evo 2 reads the whole window as context, and repeats are cached.

Waiting for Evo 2…
Evidence level: calculated. Evo 2 7B (StripedHyena 2), one forward pass per sequence, bfloat16, full-vocabulary softmax. Repeat scoring was bit-identical on this machine (15 Sep, 1,000 bp). Each value at a position is predicted from the bases before it only; the first base has none. Evo 2 computes the whole window at once in 16-bit floating point, so any change nudges every value slightly, even before the edit (measured 15 Sep: about 0.015 bits per base on average, occasionally up to 0.5). After each edit the page reports that floor. The lens only changes what you see; Evo 2 always reads the whole window. Sequence: ECOLAC from the evo2 package's test prompts. Reading frames are found by a standard-code scan on this page; gene names are not asserted. Every scored sequence is logged in data/loops/01-one-base/runs.jsonl.
